# Capability and evidence matrix

Evidence baseline: October 14, 2026. These are selected demonstrated capabilities, not universal records or performance ceilings. H means human, A animal, L research prototype, and T theoretical. Source type is stated separately where needed.

Recording samples, independent channels, perceptual resolution, and task throughput are different measurements. Unknown entries represent an unestablished general specification, not zero capability.

| Area | Demonstrated capability | Quantitative anchor or resolution limit | Main limitation | Required advance | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neural readout | H-L speech, cursor, and multidimensional control | Four continuous intended-finger dimensions in one study; no whole-body resolution specification | Biology, materials, coverage, computation | Stable simultaneous control with reliable idle detection | [R01](references.md#r01), [R02](references.md#r02) |
| Non-invasive input | H-L wrist EMG and limited EEG motor imagery | EMG example uses 2 kHz acquisition; EEG example uses seconds-long trials | Signal mixing, placement drift, physiology | Useful cross-session and cross-user control | [R03](references.md#r03), [R04](references.md#r04) |
| Neural writing | H-L localized percepts and targeted modulation | Acoustic millimeter focus is not a perceptual pixel; general content bandwidth unknown | Selectivity, causal encoding, safe exposure | Independent percepts that combine during behavior | [R25](references.md#r25), [R27](references.md#r27) |
| Vision | H-L phosphenes and recognizable forms; H retinal restoration | 96-channel cortical example, some distinct percepts at 400 micrometer spacing; frequent merging | Encoding, coverage, interactions, eye movements | Dense coherent scenes across a useful field | [R13](references.md#r13), [R14](references.md#r14), [R16](references.md#r16) |
| Hearing | H cochlear speech restoration | Electrode count exceeds effective independent spectral access; task-dependent limits | Current spread, neural survival, placement, encoding | Preserve more spectral and temporal information | [R53](references.md#r53) |
| Touch | H-L useful cortical and peripheral feedback | Localized perceived fields, not a regular body-wide grid; temporal patterns matter | Encoding, selectivity, coverage, mechanics | Natural simultaneous contact, slip, texture, and shear | [R07](references.md#r07), [R08](references.md#r08), [R09](references.md#r09), [R10](references.md#r10) |
| Proprioception | H-L selected movement percepts and vibration illusions | No general artificial whole-body angular resolution or bandwidth | Distributed biology, conflicting input, system identification | Continuous position, velocity, load, and effort perception | [R11](references.md#r11), [R12](references.md#r12) |
| Vestibular sensation | H-L implant-assisted balance and experimental modulation | Three canal branches in an eight-person study; not a complete balance replacement | Anatomy, selectivity, integration, safety | Coordinate canals, otoliths, eye responses, and posture | [R17](references.md#r17), [R18](references.md#r18) |
| Temperature | H-L thermal discrimination and referred sensation | Physical dynamics depend on heat transfer and skin; neural bandwidth unspecified | Thermal engineering, physiology, power | Fast distributed comfortable feedback | [R19](references.md#r19) |
| Pain | H-L selected somatic and unpleasant percepts | No validated general safe pain-display resolution or bandwidth | Distributed biology, variability, safety | Predictable quality without harm or persistent effects | [R20](references.md#r20) |
| Motor intention | H-L several useful command dimensions | Task-specific decoding; control dimensions are not full natural movement | Context dependence, access, calibration | Concurrent whole-body voluntary control | [R02](references.md#r02), [R52](references.md#r52) |
| Movement suppression | H localized clinical nerve-block use; A-L mechanism work | T for a safe awake recreational whole-body system | Physiology, anatomy, reversibility | Preserve essential and protective function during separation | [R21](references.md#r21), [R22](references.md#r22) |
| Latency and bandwidth | H-L fast individual components and useful closed loops | 80 ms output increments differ from seconds-scale onset; no full-dive aggregate rate established | Estimation windows, synchronization, jitter, artifacts | Predictable combined-loop operation | [R28](references.md#r28), [R29](references.md#r29), [R49](references.md#r49) |
| Encoding and decoding | H-L task-specific decoders and sensory encoders | Isolated actions do not guarantee concurrent decoding | Causal identification, context, computation | State-aware closed-loop models with uncertainty | [R10](references.md#r10), [R52](references.md#r52) |
| Safety | H clinical implant experience | Small clinical cohorts and particular exposures do not establish multisensory healthy-user risk | Tissue, surgery, stimulation, failure recovery | Chronic safety of the actual combined system | [R32](references.md#r32) |
| Adaptation | H-L longitudinal percept maps and adaptive decoders | Years of map stability differ from continuous-use safety; online and retrospective durations differ | Biological drift and coupled learning | Separate learning from drift and retain agency | [R09](references.md#r09), [R30](references.md#r30) |
| Peripheral and spinal access | H-L prosthetic sensation and movement assistance | Selected anatomical pathways; no arbitrary whole-body sensory replacement | Mixed fibers, broad recruitment, chronic access | Selective useful access while preserving function | [R23](references.md#r23), [R24](references.md#r24) |
| Invasiveness | H-L surface, endovascular, and penetrating interfaces | Preclinical delivery and intraoperative human use are separate claims | Surgical, vascular, tissue, and packaging limits | Function-specific benefit that justifies risk | [R33](references.md#r33), [R35](references.md#r35) |
